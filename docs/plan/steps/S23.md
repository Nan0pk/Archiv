## Why this step exists

The third stream, and the reason video is cheaper than it looks: Archiv already
has the right abstraction and is not using it for this. Milestone 3 built archive
recursion with a `containment` hierarchy, where a ZIP member becomes a first-class
content-addressed object whose parent is the archive. A keyframe is the same shape.

Sample on scene change, register each keyframe as a contained child object of the video,
and the existing OCR, image index and face clustering all work on them with no new
pipeline. That is what recovers the shared slide, the filmed whiteboard, or the document
held up to the camera.

Ceilings matter more here than anywhere else in the project: one hour of footage can
produce thousands of keyframes, each triggering OCR, an embedding and a face scan. Cap
keyframes per minute, total keyframes per object, decoded bytes and wall-clock — and make
exceeding a cap a clean `degraded` outcome that keeps the preserved original and the
transcript, never a hard failure.
