# S10A — Blocked: `archiv images duplicates` needs a product decision, not a threshold

**Phase B** · depends on: S10 · **blocked on the repository owner**

> Rewritten 2026-09-06. This step originally proposed a "spread guard". That proposal is
> withdrawn: the measurement it was to be built on refutes it. Nothing here should be
> implemented until the owner picks one of the three options at the bottom.

## The defect, unchanged

`archiv images duplicates` reports three clearly different generated document pages — an
invoice, a contract of sale, a medical report — as **one duplicate group** at 0.9999. A
person acting on that output deletes two unrelated documents.

## Why the proposed fix cannot work

The original proposal was: refuse to report groups when the similarities across the
candidate set are bunched too tightly to carry information. Measured spread across a whole
corpus:

| Corpus | Images | Pairs | Spread |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 different documents, 3 copies each | 15 | 105 | 0.0002 |
| 5 different outdoor scenes, 3 copies each | 15 | 105 | 0.0048 |
| 6 distinct palettes, 3 copies each | 18 | 153 | 0.2710 |
| **2 copies of one image (correct to group!)** | **2** | **1** | **0.0000** |

The last row kills it. A corpus where grouping is *correct* — everything genuinely is a
copy — produces the same tiny spread as a corpus where the scorer is *blind*. Those two
cases are indistinguishable in this signal, so a guard built on it must either refuse
real duplicate sets or admit false ones. There is no threshold, because the information
is not in the number.

## What the score actually is, and what it cannot become

Colour and edge statistics. It has no access to what a document says or how its blocks are
laid out. Two light pages of dark text have nearly identical colour statistics whatever
their content, so no amount of re-measuring, re-thresholding or re-guarding will make this
metric separate an invoice from a medical report. Tuning it further is polishing a blind
spot.

## These are three different jobs, and each has a known solution

Archiv collapsed them into one score. They are not one problem:

| Job | Known solution | What Archiv already has |
|---|---|---|
| Exactly the same file | Content hash | **Already done** — `_store_original` decides `duplicate` from the database by SHA-256 |
| Same photo, resized or recompressed | Perceptual hash (pHash/dHash), compared by Hamming distance | Nothing, but Pillow is already a dependency and no weights are needed |
| "Looks like this" | A real image embedding (CLIP-style), run locally | Nothing. Would need pinned weights with a recorded SHA-256, as `visual_ocr.py` does |
| Same document, two scans | Compare extracted text; or a page fingerprint after binarising; or layout | **Text is already extracted** — normalized segments are in the search index |

Two of the four are already solved or nearly free. The colour-cosine score is the wrong
tool for three of them and was never needed for the first.

## The decision, which is the owner's

1. **Remove.** Delete duplicate detection, and stop calling the other command
   `find-similar` when it finds near-copies of colourful photographs. Record this as
   reversing `docs/plan/DECISIONS.md` §6.
2. **Refuse.** Keep it, but decline to report groups on any corpus where the method is not
   known to work, and say so. Safe — nothing gets deleted wrongly. Costs: real duplicate
   paperwork is never found, and the refusal test is itself unreliable for the reason
   above.
3. **Change the method.** Perceptual hashing for photographs, extracted text for
   documents, and refuse where neither applies. This is the only option that makes the
   feature true, and it is a larger piece of work than one step.

Recommendation on the evidence: **3**, done as separate steps per job, because two of the
four rows above are already free — and until then, **2** for the duplicates surface
specifically, because it is the one that gets people to delete files.

## Acceptance

```
docs/plan/decisions/S10A-image-similarity-method.md
```

The deliverable of this step is the decision, written down — not code. Implementation
steps get filed from it once its shape is known, because writing acceptance targets for a
design nobody has settled is how the spread guard nearly got built.

The decision record should state which of the three options was chosen, why, and what
happens to the two commands in the meantime.
