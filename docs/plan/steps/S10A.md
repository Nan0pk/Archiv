# S10A — Stop reporting unrelated documents as duplicates

**Phase B** · depends on: S10

> Filed while doing S10, from a measurement taken during it. Not folded into S10, whose
> declared scope is the search surface, but it is a live defect and should be taken next.

## The defect

`archiv images duplicates` names images as duplicates of each other. Given three clearly
different generated document pages — an invoice, a contract of sale and a medical report,
with different text and different line layouts — it reports **one group containing all
three**, each at 0.9999 similarity to the lead.

A person acting on that output deletes two unrelated documents. That is worse than a bad
ranking: a ranked list invites judgement, a duplicate group invites a decision.

## Why the obvious fix does not work

`find_near_duplicates` uses `threshold: float = 0.95`, which nobody measured. Raising it
does not help. Measured during S10:

| Content | Lowest true match | Highest unrelated |
|---|---|---|
| Images that differ in colour | 0.9994 | 0.9000 |
| Light pages of dark text | 1.0000 | 1.0000 |

There is no threshold between 1.0000 and 1.0000. The embedding is dominated by global
colour statistics and two light pages of dark text have nearly identical ones.

## What to change

Refuse rather than guess. When the similarities across the candidate set are bunched too
tightly for the ranking to carry information, report no groups and say why — the same
shape as every other refusal in this project.

Measured during S10, and the figure to build the guard on: document pages span about
0.0007 across the whole set while colour-diverse images span about 0.23. Those are three
orders of magnitude apart, so a threshold on the spread is defensible in a way that a
threshold on the similarity is not. Measure it properly during the step rather than taking
0.0007 and 0.23 as given: they come from three groups of four generated images.

Also settle what `--threshold` means once a guard exists, since a caller passing a low
threshold is asking for exactly the behaviour the guard prevents.
